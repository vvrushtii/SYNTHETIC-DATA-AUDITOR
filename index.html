Utility-retention formula and limitations

Higher-is-better metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, F1, ROC-AUC, R²): retention = TSTR ÷ TRTR × 100.
Lower-is-better metrics (RMSE, MAE): retention = TRTR ÷ TSTR × 100. Zero or non-positive denominators are shown as N/A.

  • Retention can exceed 100% — that means the synthetic-trained model scored higher on this split, not that synthetic data is “better”.
  • It describes one model family, one split and one seed. Do not compare retention between different tasks or metrics.
  • A single score can hide poor behaviour on minority classes — read the per-class table.
  • Good utility says nothing about privacy.

How to Interpret These Results
  • ROC-AUC is the chance that a random member scores higher than a random non-member. 0.5 = near-random discrimination for this attack and setup. It does not prove that no privacy risk exists — another attack, setup or auxiliary information may succeed.
  • TPR @ 10% FPR: the share of members flagged when 10% of non-members are wrongly flagged. Chance level is 0.10. Higher means a stronger attack signal at that operating point; it is not a breach probability.
  • Membership evidence: a “proxy” setup uses labels the generator never saw, so it cannot show that real members are safe. Prefer a membership column or an attested ordered split.
  • Exact-match excess: copies above what real data collides with naturally. Near-copies are not covered.
  • Not applicable / Not Run / Failed are different states: not applicable = not available in this version; Not Run = not selected; Failed = it ran and errored. None is ever counted as a pass.
